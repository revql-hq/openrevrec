# Forms assumptions review: implementation status

This tracks the September 22 screen-by-screen review. "Addressed" means the specific observed failure has a working path and regression coverage where accounting behavior changed. "Partial" means the remaining boundary is still real; a warning or a label is not accounting support.

| Finding | Status | Current boundary or next work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Account mapping | Partial | Four required journal roles are not a four-account limit: reusable profiles, contract overrides, and obligation overrides can route to multiple GL codes. A contract profile supplies balance and billing dimensions; obligation profiles can independently route revenue accounts and dimensions. The journal stays balanced overall, and dimension-level imbalance is flagged for close review. An optional reviewed list of exact approved account-dimension combinations now checks journal lines; invalid listed combinations block close, while uncovered accounts require review. This local preflight does not simulate ERP account structures, date-effective rules, or posting. Interunit entries and obligation-level contract balance splits remain unsupported. |
| 2. Mapping transitions | Partial | Opening balance transfer, external reconciliation, and historical-account runoff are explicit. Runoff carries each contract asset or deferred balance by account and dimensions across months; the accountant records a dated closing allocation with a rationale, and missing allocations block close. The Journal and workbook label provisional allocations as draft. A later transfer can move every retained old route. A zero-balance route starts using the new mapping directly. External transfers and recorded runoff splits still require reconciliation to the actual ledger; the app does not infer the release order or reconstruct pre-cutover account history. |
| 3. Workspace setup | Partial | Desktop setup asks for company, reporting currency, account-effective month, and defaults. Contract-level monthly cutover is available; fiscal calendar, FX, and parallel books remain outside scope. |
| 4. Opening positions | Partial | Home filters by active service terms or outstanding position. A contract can enter a reconciled monthly opening position through its form or workbook; pre-cutover periods are excluded, and a declared cutover requires the opening before further activity. A per-period source identity manifest can identify omitted active contracts or current-month invoices when independently supplied. Pre-cutover transaction completeness and complex historical modifications still need review. |
| 5. Customer identity | Addressed | Customer choice is deliberate; source-system references are unique within their source. |
| 6. Contract defaults | Partial | Prices/SSP are blank, template is deliberate, annual end is inclusive and correct. Cancellable and evergreen contracts record an assessed accounting end, rationale, trigger, and optional review date. A named reviewer can document an unchanged conclusion and schedule the next review without a financial amendment; a changed assessment still requires a dated amendment. An indefinite term, automatic renewal, and automatic reassessment remain unsupported. |
| 7. Allocation exceptions | Partial | Variable, usage, and credit components can target current obligations with a rationale and exact reconciliation. A variable or usage amount can target one month within one time-based series obligation. Prospective amendments retain earned targeted revenue and allocate each component's remaining amount to unsatisfied targets, with the component trace visible in reports. Later estimate changes to an unchanged pre-modification variable or usage promise follow its original allocation, with an already-delivered share caught up in the change month. Revised consideration for already satisfied targets, an initial legacy opening with period targeting, targeted prospective treatment after unallocated opening or adjustment amounts, and ambiguous original-promise or satisfaction-path changes still need separate treatment. |
| 8. Recognition entry | Addressed | Exact days, equal touched months, and normalized calendar-month proration are distinct choices with entry previews; activity measures show prior/proposed values. |
| 9. Material rights | Partial | Exercise before later time-based or point-in-time delivery is recorded separately from satisfaction; unexercised rights still recognize at expiry. An exercised right can be linked to one same-customer renewal contract with matching delivery dates and a confirmed new-consideration-only price. Contract view and export reconcile the old right allocation with the new price and monthly revenue without adding it twice. Asset/deferred balances remain separate and any offsetting position needs presentation review; amended or differently dated renewals and changing an exercised right remain unsupported. |
| 10. Usage | Partial | Finite-unit usage remains available. A separate right-to-invoice path supports one uncapped service when the accountant documents why invoice value corresponds directly to value delivered. A dated rate change retains the earlier unit price for earlier delivery and requires a renewed accounting conclusion. Each month's values are summed and rounded once; no future units are forecast. Minimum commitments, tiers, overages, non-calendar billing periods, mixed obligations, opening positions, and other amendments remain unsupported. |
| 11. Corrections | Addressed | Source facts can be corrected without rewriting history, including imported facts. Negative billing credits link to a workspace invoice or an external original invoice reference. |
| 12. Balance classification | Partial | Journal screen labels contract asset as simplified revenue less billing. Unconditional receivables and cash-before-invoice events remain outside the model. |
| 13. Modifications | Partial | Treatment is deliberate, empty-obligation termination is allowed, and date edits are guarded. An added fixed-price service that meets the separate-contract criteria can be linked to the original, with separate revenue and balances visible together in the contract view and export. Offsetting asset and deferred positions between the linked contracts now raise a presentation warning, without netting the contracts. A same-day change to the original contract blocks that link for mixed-treatment review. Mixed modifications, variable-price additions, and contract-combination conclusions remain unsupported. |
| 14. Temporal contract views | Addressed | Effective terms are the default, baseline remains available, and change detail shows before/after terms. |
| 15. Financial preview | Addressed | Preview focuses the effective period and shows affected periods, obligations, and journal differences. |
| 16. Revenue screen | Addressed | Unscheduled revenue is visible; horizon and customer filters limit the matrix; calendar-month scope is explicit. |
| 17. Scenarios | Partial | Comparison lists proposals and accounting detail. Rebase ignores descriptive edits and permits two distinct, identified positive invoices on the same contract to combine. It also permits one identified credit linked to an older workspace invoice to combine with a distinct new positive invoice; both rebased paths preserve the original invoice link and accepted journal balance. An unreferenced invoice, unidentified credit, credit linked only to an external original reference, duplicate identity, two credits, correction, contract treatment change, or account-policy change still requires conflict review. More complex accounting conflicts remain unsupported. |
| 18. Journal handoff | Addressed | Posting guide, account detail, stable batch ID, and user-entered external journal references are available. A revised close now has a balanced replacement delta against each recorded posted checkpoint in the journal screen and workbook. The accountant must confirm which batch is in the ledger; the app does not post or verify it. |
| 19. Close controls | Addressed | Independent billing and GL totals, optional close cutoff, scenario relevance, period-linked tasks, and accepted exceptions are in place. A separate source contract and invoice manifest compares identities, exposes missing/unexpected/duplicate/unidentified rows, and preserves the accepted comparison in the close. The accountant remains responsible for the independent extract and its population filters. |
| 20. Close enforcement | Addressed | Blocking checks prevent close and every review item needs a recorded reason. Prior-period catch-up policy remains a separate design question. |
| 21. Import acceptance | Addressed | Read-only preview, atomic commit, source identity, duplicate detection, and stale-preview guard are in place. |
| 22. Import usability | Addressed | Structured amendment/correction sheets, command ordering, and downloadable row errors are in place. |
| 23. Change explanation | Addressed | Term comparisons, correction links, and broader reference search are available. |
| 24. Evidence | Addressed | Existing files can be reused and a ZIP includes the workbook and indexed files. A named reviewer records a conclusion, basis, and supported or exception disposition for each judgment change; file presence alone never clears close review. The recorded conclusion remains the accountant's responsibility. |
| 25. Dashboard attention | Addressed | A compact warning count opens the focused Accounting warnings report. Calculation warnings carry source contract and obligation IDs into close review and export, so identically worded warnings on different contracts remain separate, count separately, show distinct contract IDs, and navigate to the right records. The close review uses the same actionable warning rows. A linked-balance warning can open both contracts. Tasks retain their direct completion and contract actions. |
| 26. Main-panel footer | Addressed | The status tray occupies the bottom row of the main panel, outside the scrolling content area, and remains at the bottom while the panel content scrolls. |

The [pilot boundary cases](accounting-boundary-cases.md) specify the examples and expected outputs to collect before extending the remaining calculations. The next accounting-model work should address ambiguous original-promise changes, mixed modifications, linked-contract balance presentation, and more complex metered pricing. Legacy population completeness and historical amendments also require a migration review even when opening balances reconcile. Until then, the entry screens must keep these boundaries visible. [AASB 15 paragraphs 21, 73, 84–90, B16, and B39–B43](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-dec-2022) distinguish prospective treatment, the relative-SSP default, criteria for allocating variable consideration to particular obligations or services in a series, the invoice-value expedient, and accounting for option rights; the product captures the accountant's conclusion rather than inferring it from a price label.

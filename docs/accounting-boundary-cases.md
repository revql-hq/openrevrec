# Accounting boundary cases for pilot review

The [screen-by-screen status](assumptions-progress.md) distinguishes supported behavior from partial support. These examples identify the facts a pilot accountant should bring before the remaining calculation paths are extended. They are acceptance cases, not instructions to force the facts into today's forms.

| Screen or workflow | Case to test with a real contract | Required product result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Settings / account mapping | One $1,200 contract has implementation and subscription revenue in different GL accounts and departments. The $1,200 invoice identifies only the contract. By month end, implementation has earned $600 and subscription $100, leaving a $500 net contract liability. | Assign profiles to the contract and its revenue obligations, then check the resulting account and dimension on each journal line. A reviewed list of exact approved combinations can preflight listed accounts and block invalid lines at close. If the source list is complete, select complete coverage so any unlisted account blocks close too; otherwise uncovered accounts remain under review. Revenue can use distinct dimensions while the contract's balance remains at its contract dimension. The revenue split does not determine obligation balances: assigning $600 of the invoice to each obligation gives a $500 subscription liability, while assigning all $1,200 to implementation gives a $600 implementation liability and $100 subscription asset. Both reconcile to the same $500 net contract liability. Review the invoice allocation before any analytical split, and preserve the [contract-level presentation required by AASB 15 paragraph 105](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-dec-2022). The report flags a journal that is unbalanced by dimension; confirm the destination ledger's account structure and interunit rules before claiming an ERP-ready import. |
| Settings / mapping change | A deferred account changes while existing contracts have balances, and the controller wants old balances to run off in the old account. | Choose reviewed runoff in the effective policy, then allocate the contract's closing deferred balance between every existing and current account route for each month while an old route carries a balance. Old balances may decline to zero but cannot increase. The account positions and journal movements must reconcile to the contract balance; a missing allocation is provisional and blocks close. Check the split against the historical ledger and accounting memo. A later transfer moves all retained routes; an external reconciliation still requires a separate ledger posting. |
| Workspace setup | A contract is denominated in another currency, or reporting uses a 4-4-5 calendar or multiple accounting books. | Do not convert by changing the workspace currency or relabeling calendar months. Decide the rate, remeasurement, fiscal period, and book policy before adding inputs. |
| Contract entry | A cancellable or evergreen arrangement has no fixed accounting end date. | Record the assessed enforceable end, rationale, and reassessment trigger; revise service dates through a reviewed amendment when that assessment changes. Do not substitute an arbitrary far-future end date or assume renewal automatically. |
| Contract combination | Two agreements with the same customer or related legal customers are signed at or near the same time and promise one integrated service; the invoices use the same invoice number on different legal agreements. | Document the paragraph 17 criterion and timing, enter each source agreement reference, date, and legal customer under one accounting contract, and combine their price and obligations for one schedule. A different legal customer needs a source-specific related-party rationale; the accountant verifies that relationship. Attribute each invoice and priced-usage record to its legal source agreement, then compare both agreement IDs, each external customer identity, and their activity against independent extracts. A missing or mismatched customer reference remains in close review. Migration of established agreements or a changed combination conclusion needs separate review. |
| Allocation | A bonus applies only to one service period in a multi-period series, or a specifically allocated component remains after a prospective modification. | A reviewed variable or usage amount can target one month of one time-based series obligation. For example, $1,200 base consideration over January–December plus a $120 June-only bonus yields $100 in each ordinary month and $220 in June; a $30 increase first included in July adds $30 to July as a catch-up. A prospective amendment keeps the $120 earned in June and allocates a revised base fee to the remaining distinct months. A later reassessment of that unchanged June bonus catches up on June, not the new remaining-service price pool. A completed June bonus cannot be repriced as part of another prospective amendment; changed original promises and initial legacy period targeting still need separate treatment. Test these conclusions against a real contract and memo. |
| Material rights | A renewal option is exercised June 15 for service delivered July–December. The renewal contract begins June 15 and ends the following January, but its only service obligation runs July–December; it adds a $300 fee to a $600 allocation already carried by the original right. | Record the exercise for July–December delivery, enter the renewal contract with only the $300 new price, and link them. The broader legal term is allowed because the renewal service stays inside the right's delivery window. The support shows $900 combined consideration and, for equal monthly delivery, $100 old-right plus $50 new-price revenue each month. January has no renewal revenue. The contracts' asset and deferred balances remain separate; review any offsetting position before posting. Renewal services outside the right's delivery window and changes to an exercised right need separate treatment. |
| Usage | A customer owes a rate per unit with no contracted unit cap, possibly with a dated rate change, tiers, or overages. | One service has a separate right-to-invoice path when the accountant documents why invoice value directly corresponds to delivered value. A flat unit rate and reviewed dated rate changes calculate value from actual units: 1,001 January units at $0.015 and 9 at $0.017 earn $15.17 after monthly rounding. Alternatively, each usage fact may carry a reviewed, externally priced invoice-value amount and source reference. For example, a pricing source can value those two deliveries at $15.17 and $0.17, earning $15.34; the engine does not derive those amounts or post an invoice. No future volume is forecast. Minimums, automatic tier/overage pricing, non-calendar billing periods, other obligations, legacy openings, and other amendments still need a reviewed model before use. |
| Billing / balances | An invoice creates an unconditional receivable before service, or cash arrives before an invoice. | Keep receivable, cash, and contract asset or liability distinct, with a specified external billing and posting workflow. Today's contract asset is simplified revenue less billing. |
| Modifications | An amendment adds a distinct $300 service to a $1,200 annual service and also discounts the original service's remaining price by $120 at July 1. The net price increase is $180, below the added service's $300 standalone price. | Do not link a separate $300 contract: the whole amendment fails the separate-contract price test. If every remaining service is distinct from past service, record one prospective modification with $1,380 revised lifetime consideration and both obligations. Preserve $600 recognized through June; allocate the $780 remaining price using remaining SSP, giving $520 to the original service and $260 to the added service. July revenue is $86.67 on each, or $173.34 combined. A qualifying separate-contract amendment still uses the link and keeps schedules and balances distinct; offsetting balances raise a presentation warning under [AASB 15 paragraph 105](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-dec-2022). |
| Mixed modification | A $1,000 contract allocates $800 to integrated implementation and $200 to future distinct support. Implementation is 50% complete and has earned $400 when a July amendment raises fixed consideration to $1,200. The amended scope means the same work is now 40% complete. | If the accountant concludes under [AASB 15 paragraph 21(c)](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-dec-2022) that implementation remains non-distinct from work already delivered while support is distinct, record one mixed amendment with a reviewed $900 lifetime allocation to implementation, $300 to support, and revised implementation completion of 40%. The revised cumulative $360 produces a $40 downward July catch-up from the $400 already earned. A later 50% progress fact earns another $90, giving $50 net July revenue; support and later progress recognize the rest. Allocation rows must sum to $1,200. This path requires fixed consideration, retained original obligations, and no earlier accounting change or opening; a different recognition method or later remeasurement needs separate review. |
| Journal handoff | A closed batch was posted, then reopened and revised. | The journal screen and workbook now compare each recorded posted checkpoint with the revised batch at account and dimension detail and produce a balanced delta. Confirm which prior batch represents the ledger, particularly if several posting references exist; the app cannot verify the actual ledger. |
| Cutover / close | A legacy workbook omits a contract or source invoice, two invoice amounts, two contracts' opening balances, or two obligations within one contract have offsetting errors, a cutover completion measure differs despite matching recognized revenue, or a priced-usage import misses a source line or carries the wrong amount, while aggregate totals still reconcile. | Supply the independent contract register, current-month invoice/credit identities and signed amounts, externally priced usage IDs, delivered units, and amounts, cutover opening balances per accounting contract, and mapped obligation-level recognized amounts and cumulative measures in Source population. Investigate missing, unexpected, duplicate, unidentified, unverified, or mismatched rows even when aggregate totals match. ID-only invoice and priced-usage lists, older manifests without opening balances, multi-obligation openings without source splits, and measure-driven openings without source measures remain under review where applicable. This does not reconstruct pre-cutover history or verify the extract's own completeness or obligation mapping. |
| Cutover correction | After August billing has been entered, the accepted July opening is found to have omitted a June source adjustment. | Correct the original opening with its new source and a complete reconciled replacement. Keep July 1 as cutover, preserve both change sets, recalculate July and August, and show the corrected opening in the workbook. Main requires every affected closed month to be reopened first; a revised posted journal needs separate ledger reconciliation. |
| Corrections / close | A September invoice error is discovered after September closes, and policy calls for an October catch-up rather than a September restatement. | Capture economic and posting periods separately only after the policy for recognition, rollforward, comparative reports, and ledger export is specified. Current behavior requires explicit reopen for a change to a closed period. |
| Scenarios / corrections | Two reviewers separately correct different identified positive invoices or usage source rows on one contract, record progress on different obligations or on separate dates for one obligation, or change a metered unit rate on different effective dates, while Main advances. | Rebase invoice and usage corrections only when their original and replacement source identities remain distinct across branches. Cumulative progress or milestone facts, including corrections, can rebase when they target different obligations. Progress measurements on one obligation can rebase when original and replacement dates and source identities remain distinct; replay the combined cumulative measure by date. Same-obligation milestone, same-day progress, and same-target correction changes stay in conflict review. Separate dated rate changes and corrections can rebase when their old and new effective dates and imported source identities do not overlap; recalculate usage valuation and the balanced journal before applying. A shared invoice or usage target, duplicate replacement identity, unidentified invoice or usage row, or same-day or same-target rate change stays in conflict review. |
| Evidence | A signed agreement is linked but does not support the selected treatment, or a documented internal memo does. | A named reviewer now records the conclusion, support memo, disposition, and exception reason independently of file presence. The recorded review remains a human accounting judgment. |

For a pilot, collect one anonymized contract and its approved accounting memo, legacy schedule, invoices or usage source, chart-of-accounts mapping, and posted journal for each applicable row. Write the expected obligation allocation, monthly revenue, contract balance, and journal lines before changing the calculator. A case that cannot be stated at that level remains an explicit product boundary.

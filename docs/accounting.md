# Accounting behavior

These are the calculation conventions in the current prototype. Commercial facts and accounting conclusions are separate inputs: billing does not establish satisfaction, usage does not automatically change an estimate of consideration, and the software does not choose a modification treatment for you.

## Multiple source agreements

When an accountant concludes that two or more agreements with the same customer were entered into at or near the same time and satisfy an [AASB 15 paragraph 17](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-dec-2022) criterion, create one accounting contract with every legal agreement reference and date. Record whether the agreements form a package with one commercial objective, have interdependent consideration or performance, or promise one performance obligation, and explain the timing and conclusion. Put the combined consideration and obligations in that single contract; its revenue schedule and net balance are calculated once. Billing and usage facts identify their own source agreement, so the independent register, invoice, and priced-usage comparisons retain legal agreement identities. The workbook exports the combination conclusion and source agreements. The application does not infer whether agreements qualify or impose a fixed interpretation of “at or near the same time.” The conclusion is fixed after creation; related-party customers recorded under different customer IDs, historic conversion, and a later revision of the combination conclusion need separate review.

## Amounts and allocation

Enter money as decimal strings. The engine uses decimal arithmetic, rounds to cents, and allocates transaction price using relative standalone selling prices. Rounding residuals use the largest remainder, with stable obligation IDs breaking ties, so the allocated cents reconcile exactly to the transaction price.

Eligible variable, usage, or credit components may carry `allocation_scope: "specific"`, one or more `target_obligation_ids`, and an `allocation_rationale`. The untargeted remainder follows relative SSP across all obligations; each specifically allocated component follows relative SSP only within its selected obligations. A variable or usage component may also set `target_period: "YYYY-MM"` for one time-based service obligation when the accountant concludes that the variable amount relates to a distinct service month within a series. That amount is recognized over the intersecting days of that month under the obligation's time method; the rest of the obligation's allocation retains its original curve. A later estimate change for a completed target month is recognized as a catch-up in the change month. Component amounts must be stated in cents and the combined allocation must reconcile to transaction price. The accountant remains responsible for concluding that the targeting criteria are met.

For a prospective modification of distinct remaining services, the engine preserves earned revenue by targeted component and service month. It allocates only each component's unrecognized revised amount among its remaining targets, with the residual relative-SSP pool allocated across remaining obligations. The contract Allocation screen and workbook show each targeted component's cumulative recognized amount. A modification cannot prospectively reverse earned targeted consideration or reprice an already satisfied target. An opening position or prior obligation-level adjustment does not supply reliable component attribution, so prospective targeting remains gated in those cases.

A later reassessment of variable or usage consideration promised before a prospective modification follows the original allocation when the component amount and allocation scope were unchanged by that modification and the original satisfaction path is still identifiable. The change uses the original SSP weights or the original specific obligation/service-month target. Its already-satisfied share catches up in the reassessment period; the remainder follows original service delivery. The Allocation screen and workbook show each original-promise change by component and obligation. Subsequent prospective changes to other consideration keep this adjustment separate from the modified-contract price pool. If the amendment changed the variable promise, its original target or satisfaction path cannot be identified, or a later amendment tries to replace a reassessed amount, the engine requires a separate reviewed treatment. Opening positions with an initial period-targeted component remain unsupported.

Fixed consideration uses its stated amount. A constrained variable component uses `included_amount`; credit components use negative amounts. The transaction price is the amount included in the accounting conclusion, not the maximum possible commercial amount.

## Satisfaction methods

Start and end dates are inclusive.

| Method | Calculation |
| --- | --- |
| Exact days | Allocated consideration across actual calendar days in the service term. |
| Equal touched months | Equal weight to every calendar month touched by the service term. Partial first and last months each count as one month; changes inside a month use the elapsed share of that month's service interval. |
| Prorated calendar months | Each month's weight is its serviced days divided by that calendar month's days; the weights are normalized so the total allocation is recognized by the end of the service term. A full middle month has weight one. |
| Point in time | Recognize on the recorded satisfaction milestone. |
| Progress | A recorded cumulative percentage establishes satisfaction through that effective date. |
| Usage | Incremental recorded quantities establish satisfaction against the obligation's total units. |
| Milestone | Recorded cumulative percentages establish satisfaction; a completion milestone defaults to 100%. |

Usage, progress, and milestone obligations do not invent future activity. Until that activity is recorded, the report can show remaining consideration with no assumed future recognition date.

An uncapped metered service has a separate right-to-invoice path for one service obligation. The accountant must document why the amount entitled to invoice corresponds directly to value delivered, as required by [AASB 15 B16](https://standards.aasb.gov.au/aasb-15-dec-2022). With `metered_value_mode: "unit_rate"` (the default), actual delivered units use the positive rate effective on each delivery date; a reviewed rate change applies prospectively to later usage. With `metered_value_mode: "invoice_value"`, each usage fact carries a nonnegative, cent-precision `invoice_value` and a pricing-source `reference`. The engine recognizes that supplied amount for the delivered units, allowing a separately priced tier or overage amount after external pricing and review. It does not calculate tier thresholds, minimums, or overages from units, infer the B16 conclusion, forecast future volume, or create a billing entry from usage. Record the issued invoice separately. Both modes aggregate actual usage by calendar month; externally priced usage requires no unit rate or rate-change activity. Mixed obligations, minimum commitments, non-calendar billing periods, legacy openings, and amendments remain outside this path.

For externally priced usage, include each source usage record ID, delivered quantity, and amount in the independent monthly Source population list. The close comparison identifies missing, unexpected, duplicate, and mismatched records. An older ID-only row remains under review because matching its reference does not verify the units or amount. The reviewer must establish that the independent pricing extract includes all delivered units and that its cutoff matches the accounting period; matching data does not make the B16 judgment automatically.

A negative billing amount is a credit memo and must identify the original invoice or an external original-invoice reference. It changes billed-to-date balances, not the transaction price. A correction to a mistaken billing source fact uses the separate append-only correction command.

## Opening positions for established contracts

Create the contract with its current accounting consideration and obligations, then record an opening position before any post-cutover activity. The cutover must be the first day of a calendar month after the contract began. Enter cumulative recognized revenue for every obligation, its cumulative progress or units where the method needs a measure, cumulative legacy billing, and the independently accepted contract asset or deferred revenue balance. The form and workbook require recognized revenue less billing to equal asset less deferred revenue exactly, plus a named legacy source and reconciliation rationale. A linked file can preserve the legacy workbook or close support.

The opening amount is a beginning balance, not current-month revenue or a journal to recreate old postings. Reports omit that migrated contract before cutover. From cutover onward, remaining revenue follows the current terms and recorded activity, with journals showing only the month's movements from the accepted opening balance. The balance enters under the account mapping effective in the cutover month; OpenRevRec does not invent a transfer from its prior default account. Reconcile the legacy balance to that mapped account outside the opening-position command. Source population can compare each contract's cutover date, cumulative recognized and billed amounts, asset, and deferred balance to an independently supplied opening register, catching offsetting contract errors even when GL totals reconcile. For a multi-obligation contract, supply separately mapped legacy recognized amounts by accounting obligation ID; otherwise the split remains a close review item. The check catches offsetting obligation errors within one contract when those source rows are supplied. It does not verify the mapping from legacy obligation names to accounting IDs, the register's completeness, or past amendments. Review those facts against the legacy workbook and ledger before close.

If an accepted opening proves wrong, correct it from the contract's Activity screen. The correction keeps the contract and cutover date, records the reason and complete replacement balances, and recalculates later activity. The original and corrected entries remain in change history; the workbook shows the current accepted opening and its prior change-set ID. Reopen every closed period from the affected cutover onward before correcting Main, then review the revised schedule, source comparison, and journal delta against the ledger.

Where the opening uses cumulative progress, a milestone percentage, point-in-time completion, or delivered usage units, Source population can also compare that measure with an independently supplied legacy schedule. Matching recognized revenue alone does not verify the measure that controls later recognition. A missing source measure remains under review; a differing measure is shown as an exception.

When legacy obligation names differ from the current accounting obligation IDs, retain the original row or obligation name as a source obligation reference alongside the mapped ID. The close comparison uses the mapped ID and exports the source reference. Review the mapping and any aggregation of several legacy rows to one accounting obligation against the underlying schedule; the app cannot verify that judgment from matching totals.

For a cancellable or evergreen arrangement, the original contract `end_date` is the accountant's initial assessed enforceable accounting end, not a claim that the commercial arrangement cannot renew. Record `term_basis`, `term_assessment_rationale`, and `term_reassessment_trigger`, with an optional `term_review_date`. A due review date appears in close checks and links to the contract. If the assessment is unchanged, a named reviewer can record the conclusion, supporting basis, and next planned date without changing revenue; the workbook exports that review history. If the assessed term or service dates changed, use a dated modification instead. A later term amendment supersedes an earlier unchanged review. OpenRevRec does not extend the term or recognize renewal revenue automatically. Fixed-term contracts may omit these fields.

A material right is represented as a distinct obligation with allocated consideration. If unexercised, it recognizes on expiry (`exercise_end`, or `end_date` when absent). Record a right-exercise activity inside the exercise window when the customer elects the option; choose the future delivery dates and a supported time-based or point-in-time recognition method. Exercise alone recognizes no revenue. Time-based delivery schedules the right's existing allocation across the specified delivery term; point-in-time delivery also requires a 100% satisfaction milestone on the specified delivery date. The delivery term may extend beyond the original contract end. A pre-existing direct milestone remains available for a right whose exercise and delivery occur together within the option window. Changing an already exercised right's terms requires separate review.

When a separate renewal contract captures an additional price, create it with **only new consideration**, record the original right exercise, then link the two. The contracts must have the same customer and matching delivery dates. The link records the new price assertion and rationale; it does not move or duplicate the original right allocation. The contract view and export show the original allocation, new renewal price, and their combined monthly revenue. The contracts retain separate asset and deferred balances; an offsetting position triggers a presentation warning for review before posting. This path does not determine a net contract balance, modify an exercised right, or prove from source documents that the renewal price excludes the carried allocation.

## Changes and estimates

Contract baselines are retained. Record revised accounting through dated activity with a rationale.

- **Prospective modification:** Revised consideration is the total lifetime amount, including revenue already recognized. The engine preserves recognition through the day before the change and allocates the remainder across the remaining obligation SSP and satisfaction.
- **Cumulative catch-up:** Revised terms establish the amount that should have been recognized by the effective date. The difference from the previous cumulative amount is recorded on that date.
- **Mixed modification:** For fixed consideration with no earlier opening position or accounting change, the accountant records a reviewed revised lifetime amount and treatment for every retained or added obligation. A partially satisfied, non-distinct obligation catches up to its revised cumulative amount; if its progress-method completion measure changes with the amended scope, the accountant enters the revised cumulative percentage on that obligation's allocation. Distinct remaining service recognizes its allocation prospectively; a completed original obligation retains its earned amount. The allocations must equal revised lifetime consideration. Changing the catch-up obligation's recognition method or making later accounting changes needs a separate reviewed treatment.
- **Variable consideration reassessment:** The new included amount changes the transaction price and produces the cumulative adjustment required on its effective date.
- **Adjustment:** A signed revenue adjustment is recognized on its effective date. The remaining amount is spread over remaining satisfaction. An adjustment after full satisfaction can leave a residual that the report flags for review.

A separate-contract conclusion is entered as a new contract and linked to the original when the added promises are distinct, the net price increase reflects their standalone selling prices, and the original terms are unchanged. If an amendment reprices the original service and the whole amendment fails that separate-contract test, enter both original and added obligations in one prospective modification when all remaining services are distinct from transferred service. The revised lifetime consideration includes amounts already recognized; only the remaining amount is reallocated. Use mixed treatment only when the distinct and non-distinct effects can be identified and the accountant can support an explicit allocation to every obligation; variable prices, earlier accounting changes, and later remeasurement of that mixed amendment still need separate review. For a termination or other amendment, record the revised consideration and obligations explicitly and review the resulting scenario before accepting it.

## Period review views

Reports are calculated from the same accepted state as schedules and journals. Close readiness checks journal balance, allocation, independent source and GL control totals, source agreement and invoice identities, signed invoice amounts, priced-usage values, calculation warnings, tasks due or assigned to the period, entries recorded after the chosen close cutoff, accounting scenarios affecting the period, recognition coverage, and recorded reviews for judgment changes. Source population compares an independently prepared agreement register, invoice/credit list with signed amounts, and priced-usage extract against workspace records; equal totals alone do not clear it. An open-period invoice list without line amounts remains under review. Previously accepted close checkpoints keep their accepted result; their export marks older identity-only invoice comparisons as not amount-checked. Each check has an **Open** action leading to its underlying record. The close dialog reads the same readiness result. Review items require a recorded acceptance reason before close.

Initial contract conclusions, opening positions, right exercises, modifications, variable-consideration reassessments, manual adjustments, policy changes, and reopen actions need an explicit judgment review. Its named reviewer records the conclusion, basis, and supported or open-exception disposition. The latest disposition controls the close review item. Files linked to the exact change set remain visible and indexed separately; a file alone does not clear review, and a documented internal memo can support a review without a file. A recorded review is a human assertion, not independent verification of correctness. Activity and contract history open a change detail showing the recorded rationale, treatment, before and after reports, financial effect, review, and linked files.

The contract balance rollforward derives each opening net position from cumulative recognized revenue and billings before the selected month. Period revenue and billings move that position to the closing contract asset or deferred revenue. Billing versus revenue presents the same timing difference by contract.

Recognition coverage compares remaining revenue after the selected period with revenue already scheduled in future periods. Unscheduled progress and finite-unit revenue remains visible but does not by itself create a close warning, because future satisfaction facts are not forecast. Unexpected schedule gaps and unrecorded point-in-time or milestone satisfaction remain review items. Scenario impact shows active proposals against Main, including affected recognition periods and whether Main has advanced beyond a scenario's base version.

## Balances and journals

Revenue and billing are measured separately. The monthly net contract position determines the simplified contract asset or deferred revenue balance. Journals use four required **roles**, not a limit of four GL accounts: effective-dated workspace defaults, reusable profiles, contract overrides, and obligation revenue overrides can route those roles to many account codes. A profile can map any subset of roles and carry named dimensions such as department or project. A contract profile supplies dimensions for billing and balance lines; an obligation profile can route its revenue line to a different account and dimension combination. For revenue, resolution order is direct obligation account override, obligation profile, contract account override, contract profile, then workspace default. The obligation profile replaces only the dimension values it names; other dimensions inherit from the contract profile. This does not split the contract's asset or deferred balance by obligation. A balance-sheet account or contract dimension change with a nonzero prior-month balance requires an opening transfer journal, explicit external reconciliation, or reviewed historical-account runoff; a zero-balance route starts using the new mapping directly. Runoff carries each contract balance by account and dimensions, and requires the accountant to record each month's closing allocation with a rationale while an old route remains. A historical route can decline to zero but cannot increase. Missing allocations are provisional, appear as draft in the Journal and workbook, and block close. The accountant must reconcile recorded splits and any external transfer to the ledger; OpenRevRec does not choose a release order. A journal with different obligation revenue dimensions balances overall but may not balance by dimension; the report shows each imbalance and close review surfaces the warning. Confirm the destination ledger's [interunit balancing rules](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/finance/general-ledger/example-balanced-journals-interunit-accounting) or prepare separate balancing entries before posting. OpenRevRec does not generate those interunit entries. A reviewed list of exact account-dimension combinations can preflight the journal: invalid listed combinations block close, and the accountant can choose whether an unlisted account requires review or blocks close when the list is declared complete. This is a local check against the supplied source, not proof that the destination ledger will accept the entry. A later mapping does not rewrite an earlier month's journal. Workspace currency and rounding remain fixed.

The output is journal support for your ledger. A closed-period export has a stable batch ID. After posting it, you can record the external journal reference; if a reopened period changes its journal, the old reference remains visible against the old batch. The journal screen and workbook compare each recorded posted close with the current journal at contract, GL account, and dimension detail. A balanced delta is shown as draft support while the period is open and as accepted replacement support after reclose. If more than one old batch has a posting record, confirm which one represents the ledger; the comparisons are alternatives, not entries to combine. OpenRevRec does not post or verify the external journal. It does not maintain cash, collections, receivables aging, taxes, foreign exchange, or a general ledger.

## Closed periods

A close preserves a calculation checkpoint and journal. Accounting changes that would alter a closed result must not be silently accepted. Reopen explicitly, with a rationale, when a prior period needs to change. Review warnings and the journal before closing; a calculation result is not a substitute for the accounting conclusion that produced it.

Protection includes revenue by obligation in the closed month, even when a reallocation leaves the contract total unchanged. New contracts whose contract start, obligation starts, and activity all fall after a closed period can be added without reopening it. Backdated billing or recognition remains protected. The accepted checkpoint continues to supply closed-period balances, schedules, journals, and catch-up support.
